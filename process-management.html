Business Process Management

The Diagnosis

Two Maturities, Two Very Different Scores

Ask a process owner how well their process runs and the answer is usually somewhere near eighty percent. Score the same process against a structured rubric that demands evidence for every claim, and the number frequently halves. Neither party is lying.

What the Team Feels
75-80%

Operating Maturity

People do meaningful work every day. Decisions get made, customers get served, output leaves the building. By the standard of daily effectiveness, the process genuinely works.

THE GAP
What Evidence Shows
45-55%

Process Control Maturity

Work instructions absent. Designed templates not in use. KPIs named but never defined. Outcomes not verified in any retrievable record. Nothing is written down, so nothing can be relied on.

The gap is not a scoring artefact. It is the exposure. Everything holding the process together sits in people's heads, which means it does not survive an absence, a resignation, a scale-up or an audit.

Foundation

Process Architecture Before Process Documents

Organizations frequently begin by writing procedures. That produces a pile of documents with no relationship to one another. We begin with the architecture, so every process has a defined place, an owner and a reason to exist.

Business Process Framework

The top-level map of how the enterprise creates and delivers value, business unit by business unit and function by function

Process Register & Ownership

Every process identified, coded, and assigned a Strategic Process Owner and an Operational Process Owner with no blanks left

Process Maps & Swimlanes

Purpose, scope, key measurables, step-level responsibility, decision points, handoffs and linkages, mapped as work actually happens

Work Instructions, Templates & Records

The operating layer: how each step is performed, on what form, and what evidence the step leaves behind

Verification

Process Audits Scored Against a Rubric

A conversational audit produces a conversational finding, and conversational findings do not survive contact with a leadership review. We audit against a fixed scoring rubric where every point requires evidence produced in the room. The score is reproducible, comparable across processes, and trackable over time.

Each step carries fourteen points: six for the work instruction, six for template adherence, two for the data or record the step produces. Absent evidence scores zero. No credit is given for intent.

Performance Governance

KPI Systems That Survive the Second Month

Most KPI initiatives fail in the same way. A list of metric names is agreed, nobody defines the calculation, two people compute it differently, the numbers stop being trusted, and the review quietly stops happening. The failure is in the definition layer, not the dashboard.

📊

Business KPIs

Outcome measures the leadership team is accountable for. Revenue, cost, customer, asset and safety performance.

  • KPI tree linking outcome measures to the processes that move them
  • Definition, calculation method, frequency and data source for each
  • Reporting hierarchy from country through cluster to site level
  • Target setting with benchmarks rather than aspiration
⚙️

Process KPIs

Control measures owned by process owners, showing whether the process is operating as designed before the outcome shows it.

  • Two to three measurables per process, defined in the process map itself
  • Explicit start point, end point, formula, target, owner and frequency
  • Adherence and cycle-time measures rather than activity counts
  • Review cadence with a named forum and an escalation path

One Template, Every Business Unit

We build a single KPI definition and tracking structure that every business unit populates the same way. Comparability across units is the point. When each unit invents its own format, consolidation becomes a monthly reconciliation exercise and the leadership review turns into an argument about numbers instead of a discussion about performance.

Support Scaled to Your Organization

Building process infrastructure from nothing, tightening what exists, and governing it across multiple units are three distinct engagements.

Your Situation

No Process Infrastructure at All

The business runs on the founders' memory and a handful of spreadsheets. It works until it does not, usually at the point where headcount doubles or a key person leaves.

  • Nothing documented beyond a few informal notes
  • Knowledge concentrated in two or three people
  • Onboarding takes months because nothing is written
  • Same errors recurring with no mechanism to prevent them
  • Investors or customers beginning to ask about controls

How We Engage

  • High-level business process map to establish the architecture first
  • Process register with owners assigned before any document is written
  • Prioritised mapping of the ten to fifteen processes that carry the most risk
  • Lean work instructions and templates sized for a small team
  • Basic KPI set with definitions, so measurement starts correctly
  • Process owner coaching so ownership sits inside the business

Your Situation

Documented, But Not Practised

Processes exist on paper. Whether anyone follows them is another question, and nobody has a reliable way to find out. Self-assessment says one thing, reality says another.

  • Process documents written once and not revisited
  • Work instructions missing for most steps
  • Templates designed but not in use
  • KPIs named on a cover slide but never defined
  • No independent verification of what actually happens

How We Engage

  • Structured process audits producing an objective, comparable score
  • Self-assessment checklists so owners can track between audits
  • Work instruction and template development for the critical gaps
  • KPI definition workshops converting names into measurable specifications
  • Process owner training and independent evaluation
  • Audit programme design so verification continues without us
